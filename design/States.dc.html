Why this matters more than it looks
Bookly makes you unlock the phone and open the app to stop a session, which is the exact behaviour a reading app should be discouraging. This notification is ongoing and non dismissable while a session runs, survives the app being killed, and needs an Android foreground service. Budget real time for it. Tapping the body opens the session screen; the two buttons act without opening anything.
10 · MOTION
press feedback · scale .97, 120ms ease-out
screen push · slide 240ms, cubic-bezier(.2,0,0,1)
sheet up · 280ms, same curve, scrim fades 200ms
toast in · slide + fade 180ms
skeleton shimmer · 1400ms linear, infinite
One number animates on the whole home screen: the page count when a session saves, counting up over 600ms. Everything else is instant. Progress bars grow to their new width over 400ms. Respect the reduce motion setting by dropping every duration to 0 rather than writing a second set of animations.
11 · EDGES THAT BREAK LAYOUTS
Small screens
These artboards are 390px. A lot of Android stock is 360px. Every layout must survive 360 with no horizontal scroll. Test the Library row and the four quick add chips first, they are the tightest.
Long titles
Book titles run long. Two lines then ellipsis in list rows, full wrap on the detail screen. Author names get one line always. Never let a title push a button off screen.
Missing data
No cover, no page count, no author. All three are common. Covers fall back to a solid colour derived from the title, page count falls back to percent, author falls back to nothing rather than "Unknown".
Big libraries
Importers arrive with 500 to 2000 books. Lists must be virtualised from day one. Do not build a plain map over the array, it will be fine on your phone and terrible on theirs.
Touch and contrast
Nothing tappable under 44px, including the small icon buttons in headers. Body text never below 12px. The muted grey is the floor for text on either ground, do not go fainter for real content.
Keyboard
The log session and manual entry screens both put fields near the bottom. The primary button must ride above the keyboard, not hide behind it. This is the single most common Android layout bug.
